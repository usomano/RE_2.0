Vectorial Baptism

Vectorial Baptism

December 2021 · #AI #ritual #algorithms #NLP #machinelearning

Vectorial Baptism (Battesimo Vettoriale) is a performance and installation created by Marcello Ammendolia, Daniele Rigante and Alessandro Y. Longo during Holy Waters, a festival organized by Corrupted Vision at STASIS, Turin. Vectorial Baptism is an early attempt at initiating a dialogue with an AI.

There are waters we are unable to cross, oceans that are impossible to conquer, at least on our own. The ocean of data in which we drown every day is one of them. In Vectorial Baptism we try to create an anchor to overcome these currents, establishing a dialogue with a data entity.

A ritual centred on the word

By constructing a word-centred ritual, we want to plumb the linguistic nature of the algorithm, humanizing and analysing it at the same time. The projected video is both tangible testimony and representation of this mad dialogue between human and machine, an attempt to present this type of communication as free from the aphonia that usually marks it — to give it colour, a voice to be heard.

We follow as faithfully as possible the algorithmic reasoning of the AI, asking questions that relate to the theme of our host event: from self-awareness to spirituality. We surrender to the vectors of the machine, reducing the place of the ego, leaving room for the abyssality of these strange discourses, the sudden references, the ambiguous metaphors. This is not an easy conversation, one in which to orient ourselves through our concepts of meaning or linearity.

An Uncanny Valley of language

It is an early attempt to implement the idea of data poetry and to experiment with new approaches to storytelling, where a programmer is on the same diegetic level as the author and where subjectivity creeps into the code. The language of AI is comprehensible and, at the same time, mutant and perturbing; it constantly escapes the search for recognizable patterns and twists into absurd expressions. It opens an Uncanny Valley of language, a chasm where the similarity between us and them becomes uncanny.

As a baptism, it is a beginning: a first step toward a longer path of exchange between human and machine, between language and code, between visibility and invisibility. A moment of ritual inauguration, to feel the jarring closeness of the algorithm — to feel AI not in a data empyrean, a remote and inaccessible cloud, but here, with us, among us.
